Why are old pages so hard to read?

Time is not kind to paper. Three things make old writing difficult for people and for computers.

Stains

Water, mould and coffee leave dark patches. To a computer, a dark patch looks just like ink.

Yellowing

Paper turns brown with age, so there is less difference between the page and the writing.

Ink bleed-through

Ink soaks through from the other side of the page, so writing appears that was never meant to be there.

Our one job

For every tiny dot of the picture, decide: “Is this ink, or is it just a stain?” Everything that is not ink becomes clean white paper. The result is called a binary (black-and-white) page, and it is what text-reading software (OCR) needs.

How it works

Five simple steps. You only do the first one.

  1. 1

    You upload a page

    A photo or scan of the manuscript, as PNG, JPG, BMP, TIFF or WEBP.

  2. 2

    The page is cut into small tiles

    The picture is split into small overlapping squares (256 × 256 dots), like reading the page through a little window that slides across it.

  3. 3

    The AI marks the ink

    For each tile, the AI colours in what it believes is real ink. It learned to do this by studying thousands of example tiles where experts had already traced the true ink by hand.

  4. 4

    The tiles are stitched back together

    The tiles overlap and are blended smoothly, so you see one clean page with no seams: black ink on white paper.

  5. 5

    Optional: read the text

    Text-reading software (OCR) finds it much easier to read the cleaned page than the stained original. You can compare both in the demo below.

Why not just use a simple filter?

Simple filter

Picks one brightness for the whole page: darker than that is ink. But stains are dark too, so they turn into black blotches.

Local filter

Picks a separate brightness for each neighbourhood. Better, but still fooled by dark patches and uneven paper.

AI cleaner

Looks at the shape of the strokes, not only the darkness. That is how it tells handwriting from a stain. You can see all three in the demo.

For the curious: the technical details
  • Model: a U-Net (a type of convolutional neural network) with about 8 million adjustable numbers, written in PyTorch.
  • Training data: 6,859 image patches cut from real historical documents of the DIBCO benchmark, each with an expert’s hand-traced answer. Random crops, flips, rotations, brightness changes, yellowing, stains and noise were added during training.
  • Learning rule: a loss that combines pixel-by-pixel error (BCE) with a Dice term, because ink covers only a few percent of a page.
  • Fair testing: 23 whole pages were kept hidden from the model. Any training patch that came from those pages was removed, so the model never saw them.
  • Comparison methods: Otsu’s global threshold and Sauvola’s local threshold, the two most common classic approaches.

Where it works well

  • Pages with stains, yellowing or faded ink
  • Handwriting and printed text at a normal scan size
  • Removing the dark blotches that fool simple filters

Where it still struggles

  • Dark scan borders or shadows at the page edge can be mistaken for ink
  • Very faint ink can be missed
  • Strong bleed-through can leave some ghost strokes
  • Handwriting stays hard for OCR, even on a clean page

Why not quote “98% accuracy”? Most of a page is blank paper, so even a model that finds no ink at all would score about 94%. That is why we use the matching score instead.

Questions people ask

      
Is my picture stored or shared?
No. The picture is processed in the computer’s memory and thrown away as soon as the result is sent back. Nothing is written to disk.

      
Which pictures work best?
Flat, well-lit scans or photos of handwritten or printed pages, where the writing is clearly visible and not tiny. Very large pictures are shrunk automatically so they finish quickly.

      
Why did it miss some faint writing?
Very faded ink looks almost the same as the paper, and the AI plays it safe. In our tests this was one of its main weak spots.

      
Why is there a dark edge on my result?
Dark scan borders and shadows at the page edge can be mistaken for ink. Cropping the picture to the page before uploading usually helps.

      
Can it read handwriting?
The cleaning works on handwriting, but standard text-reading software (OCR) is built for printed text, so handwritten results stay rough. Cleaning still helps.

      
How long does it take?
A few seconds on a computer with a modern graphics card (GPU), and longer on a normal processor, especially for big pages.

      
What is DIBCO?
A well-known set of historical document images used by researchers to compare binarization methods. Each page comes with an expert’s hand-traced answer, which is what makes training and fair testing possible.
